1. Upon looking into the AI world advancements which are plenty and very fast growth and demand. Coming from a systems perspective I settled onto a "hardware-software" area of research which was quantization which worked on reducing the cost of resources used for running of these LLMs. Those resources are primarily GPUs and with fast growing scale (the scale problem we all know), it is getting harder to run on edge or low capacity (constrained) devices like mobile phones etc. 
2. The main area within quantization which I focussed on was PTQ (Post Training Quantization). Early on through my experiments in early stages I verified that during inference simply reducing the weight memory by reducing its precision post training would not yield expected 50% reduction in peak vram usage and it went down as I scaled up. Hence the primary bottleneck was identified as "activation memory" which was the inference intermediate calculation memory. 
3. The dettmers and smoothquant both have focussed on trying to resolve this bottleneck since merely trying to use less precision based activations (naive W8A8) has bad degradation as we scale up due to outliers.
4. Dettmers systematically observed patterns about these outliers particularly how they occured for specific channels and how a channel would have outliers for all the tokens if it had one. This also stemmed the reason for me using "per channel" weights fine grainedness even though this was for activations but since smoothing shifts quantization difficulty to weights it could also be applied there. Hence the reasoning for 1st layer modification.
5. The smoothing paper succeeded in trying to solve the problem from root by building a mathematical approach for shifting quatization difficulty towards weights in order to make them both easier to quantize. This was done as a calibration step before the dynamic quantization. But, in thier approach I noticed some gaps and scope of improvements that i expected to yield a better result in terms of accuracy and perplexity on various tasks basically showing that we can reduce the memory required for inference of some models on edge devices while also not degrading much from the full memory usage.
6. My method works by making 2 modifications on one of each step (layer) of smoothing. The upper layer is when the dynamic smoothing happens where i used "per-channel" weight scheme with "per-token" activation for reasons I gave earlier. This is the outer layer. The 2nd modification is on the inner calibration step where the percentile based smoothing I applied. Reasons you already know like the outlier that sometimes even come within a channel backed by their own problem admission in their paper where they clip top 2% activations for some models(reference paper section 5.2 "results on different llms"). This works ad hoc maybe but I developed a mathematical solution for this.
7. Hence my method is diffrent than theirs on both levels and the goal was to establish a better result on mid-range models as the resource constraint occur while testing for larger models like they did (175B), this could also benefit in edge deployment of these models.
