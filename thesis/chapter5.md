# Chapter 5 — Conclusion and Future Work

## 5.1 Conclusion

This thesis took the SmoothQuant recipe of [5] as a starting point and asked a narrow but practical question: are the calibration choices that go into building the per-channel smoothing factor `s` the right ones for the OPT family across the mid-range of model scales? The answer the work arrived at is that two of those choices can be tightened without leaving the training-free, fully-INT8, hardware-friendly envelope the original method was designed to enforce.

The first tightening replaces the per-channel `max` summary statistic with a per-channel quantile `Q_p`. The substitution is mathematically a strict generalisation — `p = 1` recovers the upstream formula bit-for-bit — but it removes the dependence of `s_j` on the single most extreme token seen during calibration. The same phenomenon that the SmoothQuant authors patched on GLM-130B with an ad-hoc top-2% clip on the quantization step is addressed here at the smoothing step itself, inside the formula, with no extra moving parts. The quantile statistic is paired with per-channel weight quantization on the outer step, which the original paper does not name on OPT but which is the natural absorber for the variation that smoothing migrates onto the weight side.

The second tightening lets the migration strength `α` vary across the layers of the network, driven by a per-layer outlier severity profile measured on the same calibration corpus. The severity profile turns out to be sharply structured — a low layer 0, a peak at layers 1–3, and a smooth decay through the rest of the network — and the same shape repeats across OPT-2.7B, OPT-6.7B, and OPT-13B with the spread between the calmest and most outlier-heavy layer growing from 13× to over 46× as the model scales. A single global `α` cannot accommodate this; a parametric `α(l)` with two scalar bounds (`α_{\min} = 0.5`, `α_{\max} = 0.9`) and zero further degrees of freedom can.

The two tightenings are orthogonal in the sense that they change different parts of the calibration pipeline, and they reach a similar accuracy floor through different routes — quantile smoothing as an intra-channel fix that removes the influence of single-token spikes, per-layer `α` as an inter-layer fix that lets layers with different severities use different migration strengths. Both are pure offline calibration interventions: nothing changes at inference time, no new operators are introduced, and the same INT8 GEMM kernels that the upstream method targets are reused unchanged. The end-to-end deployment measurement under torch-int's CUTLASS kernels confirms what the design predicts — the proposed recipe deploys in real INT8 at zero extra memory cost relative to the upstream `max`-based recipe and approaches the theoretical 2× memory reduction over FP16, with weight memory at roughly 50% of FP16 and peak VRAM at roughly 70%.

The contribution is therefore narrow but clean. It is not a new architecture and not a new training procedure. It is a reproducible characterisation of two places where the SmoothQuant calibration leaves accuracy on the table on the OPT family in the deployment-relevant mid-range of model scales, and a principled — and crucially, hardware-compatible — fix for both.

## 5.2 Future Work

Several directions follow naturally from the work in this thesis but were left out of scope by the resource constraints or the deliberate choice to keep the contribution narrow.

**Extending the real-INT8 deployment ladder past OPT-1.3B.** The fake-quant accuracy results in Chapter 4 cover the OPT family up to 13B, but the end-to-end memory measurement under torch-int currently runs only on OPT-1.3B. Pushing the same measurement through OPT-2.7B, 6.7B, and 13B is a matter of compile-time engineering rather than method design — the calibration recipe is regime-corrected by the `α`-regime split in §3.5 — but it would close the loop on the deployment claim across the whole ladder. OPT-13B in particular would land near the boundary of what fits on a single A100-40GB in INT8, which is precisely the deployment regime where the memory savings become qualitatively load-bearing rather than just quantitatively useful.

**Per-layer quantile probability `p(l)`.** The diagnostic in §3.4.1 found that the `Q_{0.99}/median` curve traces a bathtub shape across the network — high in the early layers, near 1 through the middle, rising again at the last few layers — and that this shape is partly distinct from the `max/median` shape that drives `α(l)`. The current work uses a single global `p` for the whole network. A natural next step is to let `p` itself vary with layer index, with low-`p` (more aggressive) settings on layers where the bulk of the activation distribution is broad and high-`p` (more conservative) settings on layers where outliers are confined to one or two freak channels. This would attack the second axis of the `(σ, p_{99}/\mathrm{med})` plane that the per-layer `α` modification leaves untouched.

**Decoupling activation-side and weight-side quantile probabilities.** The smoothing factor in this work uses the same `p` for both `Q_p(|X_j|)` and `Q_p(|W_j|)`. Decoupling the two — letting `p_X` and `p_W` be set independently — has not been tried, mostly because the weight-side distribution is flat enough that `Q_p(|W_j|)` is insensitive to `p`. But this is an empirical observation that has not been verified at every scale, and the decoupled version is a strict generalisation of the current formula. Confirming or refuting the insensitivity claim would either justify the single-`p` simplification rigorously or expose a new tuning axis.

**Other model families.** The OPT family was chosen because it is the family on which the SmoothQuant paper reports its primary scaling results, which gave a clean basis for comparison. The recent open-weight families — Llama-2 [18], Mistral [19], and Mixtral [20] — have qualitatively different outlier structure, with Llama-2 in particular reported to need higher `α` (0.85–0.9) than the 0.5 that suits OPT [5, Table 7]. The proposed pipeline applies to these models without modification (the smoothing identity is architecture-agnostic), but whether the per-layer severity profile takes the same shape across them, and whether the quantile statistic delivers the same robustness gain, is an open question worth measuring directly.

**Lower-bit weight quantization.** The proposed recipe is W8A8. The original SmoothQuant paper itself flagged W4A8 and W4A4 as a natural extension once smoothing has shifted enough difficulty onto the weight side to make INT4 weight quantization viable [5, Appendix A]. Combining quantile-based smoothing with a state-of-the-art INT4 weight quantizer such as GPTQ [21] or AWQ [22] is a clean follow-up: the smoothing step is unchanged, the weight quantization step swaps INT8 per-channel for INT4 per-group, and the rest of the inference path stays the same. INT4 instructions are now natively supported on NVIDIA's Hopper architecture, so the hardware path is ready.

**KV-cache quantization.** At long sequence lengths and large batch sizes, the KV cache dominates inference memory — the SmoothQuant authors themselves cite a 3 TB KV cache for batch size 512 and context length 2048, three times the size of the model weights [5, Appendix A, citing 23]. The proposed pipeline does not touch the KV cache; extending the same offline-calibration philosophy to KV-cache quantization, with quantile-based statistics gathered alongside the existing activation calibration, would close the last large remaining FP16-resident memory consumer in the inference path.

**Cleaner mathematical formalisation of `α(l)`.** The parametric form for `α(l)` used in this work is a linear interpolation between two fixed bounds, driven by the normalised severity profile. This works empirically, but a tighter analysis — for example, deriving the optimal `α(l)` from a closed-form objective on the per-layer joint quantization error of `\hat X^{(l)}` and `\hat W^{(l)}` — would replace the empirical recipe with a principled one and might generalise across model families without per-family retuning of the bounds.

None of these directions are blockers for the contribution of this thesis, and most of them are independently interesting in their own right. They are listed here as the natural neighbours of the work, in the order in which a follow-up project might pick them up.
